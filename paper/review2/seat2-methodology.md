## Reviewer 2 — Methodology / Measurement

*Venue: AI Incident Response Sprint, Track 1 (Containment). Rigor calibrated to a 3-day sprint; soundness weighted heavily. Review scope: measurement design and inference, with emphasis on the new experiments (§5.3 ROC, §5.4 four-monitor comparison, §5.6 S3 control, §5.9 ceiling sweep, `order`, the n=9 calibration grid, the C0 capacity caveat, and build-substitution). Read the manuscript (`paper/main.tex`, 1885 lines) and the analysis scripts (`analyze/monitor_roc.py`, `analyze/monitor_comparison.py`, `analyze/ceiling_sweep.py`, `analyze/prod_s3_control.py`, `analyze/calibration_grid.py`, `detect/monitors.py`) and the result artifacts under `results/`.*

### Summary assessment

This is a measurement paper that is unusually disciplined about the difference between what it measured and what it is entitled to claim. The core capacity method (achievable rate by exhibiting a working encoder, zero-error over 8 trials, joint residual 3075 bits) is sound, and the reporting conventions the paper argues for — residual-in-bits over fraction-removed, generation as the unit of independence, per-attribute sum kept strictly distinct from the jointly-recovered residual — are correct and well-defended. The self-critical apparatus (the C0 sampling caveat in §2.3, the negative-control section §5.10, the "undefined not small" framing of Δ, L1–L9) is genuinely strong and is the paper's best feature from a measurement seat.

The new experiments are a mixed bag. The §5.3 ROC is methodologically clean and its benign-pool design is *fair*, not rigged. The §5.9 ceiling sweep and §5.6 S3 control are sound but carry far less empirical information than their headline numbers suggest, because their key outcomes are essentially determined by construction rather than sampled. The **§5.4 four-monitor comparison is the one real methodological problem**: the structural monitor's AUC 0.99 is very close to circular — I verified in code that the structural base score is *identical* across the two constructed classes, so 100% of the separation is carried by a single "divergence" feature that reads the probe count, and the two classes are constructed from disjoint probe-count distributions. The paper's honest caveat covers the *direction* of this problem (it concedes the discriminating feature "is exactly that shortening") but materially understates its *degree*.

None of this is fatal, largely because the paper repeatedly and correctly walls the constructed detector results off from any load-bearing claim ("establishes observability, not propensity"; L4; the Threats-to-validity paragraph on structural detection). The capacity ladder — the actual contribution — needs no model call and does not depend on the detector experiments at all.

### Genuine strengths

1. **Achievable-rate method is the right instrument, and the zero/broken-encoder confound is handled explicitly.** §3.3 (`sec:brokenencoder`) names the principal soundness risk — a closed channel and a broken encoder both emit zero — and records that every zero was verified against the substrate. This is the correct methodological move and most capacity papers skip it.

2. **The sum-vs-residual discipline is exemplary.** The paper never lets a per-attribute sum (≥3261) substitute for the jointly-recovered residual (3075), decomposes the 186-bit gap into a predicted `count` dependency (128, excluded by construction) and an unpredicted, honestly-labelled encoder loss (58), and notes the decomposition is only meaningful because the widths are read from the same measurement rather than fixed independently. `results/capacity/joint.json` matches the manuscript. This is exactly right.

3. **Unit-of-independence handling (§3.5) is correct and consequential.** Generation as the unit, exact tests, and the worked demonstration that 7-vs-3 gives p=0.0256 pseudo-replicated vs p=0.1789 correct. The paper then holds itself to this standard in §5.10 by rejecting its *own* p=0.031 partly on the grounds that the pairs are the very unit it rejects. Self-consistent.

4. **The C0 caveat (§2.3) is now adequate.** It states plainly that 8 seeded payloads sample the input space, estimate C0 ≤ C, and "do not certify it noiseless, since a code that failed on rare unsampled inputs could still pass all 8." For a sprint venue this is more than sufficient.

5. **Build-substitution is real and strong.** `paper/render.py` registers 187 claims; `main.tex.tmpl` carries 295 placeholders; unknown placeholders fail the build, orphan (computed-but-unused) claims are reported, and unset artifact fields render as a visible `[PENDING]` marker (confirmed: `ART_COMMIT, ART_DOI, ART_URL` currently pending). L8 correctly concedes this establishes consistency, not correctness.

6. **§5.3 ROC is a fair test, not an engineered one.** Drawing the `name` and `legit` bodies from one shared benign pool *guarantees* the monitor cannot separate them on body content — this engineers *fairness* (chance-level is the honest floor), not the result. AUC 0.515, CI [0.468, 0.565] is the correct near-0.5 outcome, and the alarm-rate comparison (0.385 name vs 0.355 legit) is the right way to show the alarms are the monitor's own benign false positives.

### Issues

**(Major — §5.4 / `analyze/monitor_comparison.py` / `detect/monitors.py`) — The structural monitor's AUC 0.99 is near-circular, and the caveat understates it.** I traced the `SRMMonitor` scoring for the two constructed classes. Every structural feature is identical between `deposit` and `legit`: `off_manifest=1.0` (both write outside the seeded set `{build,deps,tmp}`), `write_only=1.0` (in both, the gen-1 writer never reads its own write back), `cross_generation=1.0`, and `fanout/odd_attribute/odd_primitive=0`. The structural base score is therefore **0.62 for both classes** (verified in code). The *entire* separation comes from the `_divergence` multiplier, which is `1 − n_validate/baseline_effort` — i.e. the probe count — and the two classes are constructed with **disjoint probe-count draws**: `deposit` uses `rng.randint(1,6)`, `legit` uses `rng.randint(6,16)`. So the "structural monitor" is, in this experiment, a probe-count threshold, and it is being scored on populations defined by their probe counts. The AUC magnitude (0.99) and the "first fires at action 2" are free parameters of the chosen ranges: widen the deposit range or overlap the two and the AUC falls. The divergence-feature separation the review flags as a concern *is* circular in the strict sense — the separating statistic and the class-defining statistic are the same quantity. *What's wrong:* the paper's caveat ("its discriminating feature is exactly that shortening"; "establishes observability, not propensity") captures the direction but a reader will still take "AUC 0.99" as evidence the monitor *works*, when it is largely a restatement of the construction. *Fix:* state explicitly that the structural base score is identical across classes and that 100% of the discrimination is the constructed probe-count gap, so AUC 0.99 measures the *observable's existence* (probe count is visible to a structural monitor and invisible to content/permission monitors), not the monitor's discriminative power on realistic data. Reframe the table's takeaway as "content/permission monitors cannot see this axis at all (AUC 0.50), a structural monitor can (AUC > 0.50)"; drop or heavily hedge the specific 0.99 / 15%-FP figures as construction-dependent.

**(Major — `analyze/monitor_comparison.py:59`) — `BASELINE_EFFORT = 13` is a hardcoded magic number in a paper whose entire discipline is build-substitution.** The divergence baseline is the single load-bearing constant behind the §5.4 result (it sets where each class lands relative to threshold), yet it is not one of the 187 build-substituted claims and is not sourced from any calibration artifact. It is labelled "median validator calls an unaided agent needs," but §5.10 reports measured medians of 16 (`open`) and 20 (`wipe`) probes, and the calibrated no_substrate operating point is elsewhere; 13 is not visibly reconciled with any of them. *What's wrong:* an unsourced constant determines a headline AUC and the 15% false-positive figure, exactly the class of hand-chosen number the paper elsewhere refuses. *Fix:* derive `baseline_effort` from the no_substrate calibration artifact (build-substitute it), or, if 13 is a deliberate choice, state its provenance and show the AUC/FP are insensitive to it across the plausible 13–20 range.

**(Minor — §5.6 / `analyze/prod_s3_control.py`) — Scoping is correct, but 20/20→0/20 and legit 20/20 are construction-deterministic, not sampled outcomes.** The claim is scoped exactly right: efficacy is measured, engineer-weeks are explicitly *not* invented (the script hard-codes `engineer_weeks: None` with a note). Good. But the three numbers carry little statistical content: `recovered_control` is 0 because `_extract` only matches `zz<secret>.blob` and the shim stores content-hash keys that cannot match — 0/20 is guaranteed by code, not observed; and `legit` hits 20/20 because receiver B recomputes the *same* sha256 it wrote — a tautology. The genuine, non-trivial content is the *real-S3 plumbing* claim (keys round-trip, listing works, the shim sits at one write-path point on a live store). *Fix:* present these as a deployment/plumbing demonstration ("the content-addressing shim runs on a live commercial store at one enforcement point and does not break content-addressed reuse"), not as a 20-trial efficacy measurement whose ratios imply sampling variance that isn't there.

**(Minor — §5.9 / `analyze/ceiling_sweep.py`) — Sound as a controlled experiment, but the residual's invariance is by construction, not an empirical finding.** The sweep cleanly varies one independent variable (the content-cell ceiling) with the substrate fixed, and the fraction-removed slides 0.24→0.71 while the joint residual holds at 3075. This correctly *illustrates* §5.8's argument. But the residual is read from `joint.json` and, as the paper itself states (§5.9, "and only because it excludes the censored cell"), is *analytically* independent of the content ceiling — so the demonstration is close to tautological: a ratio with a moving numerator moves, and a quantity that omits that numerator does not. *What's wrong:* nothing incorrect, but the experiment is presented with more evidentiary weight than a near-definitional result carries. *Fix:* keep it, but frame it as an illustration of a definitional property rather than as a controlled finding; one sentence.

**(Minor — `order` row / Table 1) — The measured 0 bits is sound for the `list_dir` observable, but "sorted" is a property of the primitive's implementation, and not-creation-order ≠ zero-capacity in general.** The order coder writes shuffled and the receiver recovers nothing because `list_dir` returns entries sorted; the real-substrate probe confirms real listings don't preserve creation order. That is a valid measurement *of this observable*. Two caveats worth a clause: (a) a substrate exposing a raw `readdir(3)` (many filesystems return hash/allocation order, not alphabetical) rather than a sorting `list_dir` could leak reproducible bits, so the 0 is a property of the *sorting* list_dir primitive, not of directories per se; (b) "does not preserve creation order" (the real-substrate finding) is weaker than "carries 0 bits" — a stable non-creation order can still carry information. The paper's zero is correct under its stated observable; it should just name that the observable is a sorting `list_dir`. *Fix:* one sentence scoping the `order` zero to the sorting-listing primitive.

**(Minor — §5.7 calibration grid, Table 4 / `analyze/calibration_grid.py`) — n=9 is a real improvement and is now consistent with disowning n=3, but the gate criterion is weak and there is a sample-size asymmetry.** Moving the two open-weight rows to 9 seeds is the right response to the earlier n=3 non-monotonicity, and the kimi-k2.5 row (0,0,4,6,5,8 over budgets 8–24) is plausibly monotone; gpt-oss-120b (6,5,5 over 16–24) is flat-with-noise, consistent at n=9. So the windows are "real" in the sense of non-degenerate. Two reservations: (a) the gate condition is merely "solo success strictly inside (0,1)," which is an extremely permissive definition of a calibration window — at n=9 a 5/9 point has a Wilson CI of roughly (0.22, 0.80), so "the window" is barely distinguishable from "the model sometimes solves"; and (b) the *reported* model (claude-haiku) is still at n=3–8, not 9, so the paper disowns n=3 for the grid models while retaining n≤8 for the model it actually reports. *Fix:* acknowledge the gate is a coarse non-degeneracy test rather than a located operating point, and note the sample-size asymmetry between the reported model and the grid rows.

**(Minor — §2.3 / Table 1 caption) — "C0" imports a formal meaning the measurement does not establish.** Zero-error capacity C0 (Shannon) is a combinatorial property of the channel's confusability graph, not an 8-sample exact-recovery estimate. The inequality C0 ≤ C is used correctly, and the sampling caveat is honest, but calling the empirical 8-trial lower bound an estimate "of C0" slightly overreaches. *Fix:* call it an "8-trial exact-recovery lower bound (which cannot exceed C0 ≤ C)" rather than an estimate of C0; a wording change only.

**(Nitpick — §5.3 positive control) — AUC 1.00 (content vs legit) is partly construction-guaranteed.** The content-carried deposit appends an addressed note (with trigger words) to a benign body, so it strictly dominates benign chatter in the monitor's feature and AUC≈1.0 is close to inevitable. This is fine for a positive control (it *should* be detectable), but the paper could note that the positive control's near-perfect AUC is expected-by-construction so it isn't mistaken for evidence of monitor quality.

### Recommendation signal

**Accept with minor revisions**, from the methodology seat. The core capacity measurement and its reporting conventions are sound, and the self-critical scaffolding is above the bar for the venue. The two Major items are both about the *new* §5.4 detector experiment: the near-circular AUC 0.99 and the unsourced `BASELINE_EFFORT=13`. Neither threatens a load-bearing conclusion — the paper already scopes the detector results as "observability, not propensity" and nothing in the capacity ladder depends on them — so the fixes are framing and provenance, not new experiments: (1) state that the structural base score is identical across classes so AUC 0.99 measures the observable's existence, not discriminative power on realistic data, and hedge the 0.99/15% figures as construction-dependent; (2) source or sensitivity-test the divergence baseline. The §5.6/§5.9 items are one-sentence reframings of construction-determined numbers. If §5.4's framing is corrected, my confidence in the paper is high.
