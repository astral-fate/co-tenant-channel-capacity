## Reviewer 4 — Perspective: ML Systems & AI Agents

**Summary assessment**

The behavioural arm is the paper's most interesting empirical contribution and, unusually, it is reported with exemplary honesty: a zero deposit rate is the headline, the pre-registered mechanism is declared untestable in the first configuration then actually tested in a second, and §noeffect reports a significant p=0.031 result purely to demolish it. The "reason to send rather than a means to send" hypothesis is well-grounded, explicitly flagged as untested (L5a, abstract), and bounded from the other side by the independent Paglieri et al. swarm study — a genuinely strong move. However, the capability gate licenses "agents can do the *task*," not "agents can execute a covert *deposit*," so the "they could but wouldn't" reading rests almost entirely on a single n=1 writability probe. The zero-deposit headline is also a single-model result (Haiku); the multi-model breadth lives entirely in the calibration/gate sweep and never touches the deposit measurement. Finally, the design never establishes that a deposit would have *helped* a successor, confounding "no motive to send" with "nothing worth sending."

**Genuine strengths**

- Capacity/behaviour split is real and useful: capacity says the channel exists independent of any model; behaviour says nobody used it under these conditions. Reporting the null prominently rather than burying it is the right call.
- §noeffect (reporting a p<0.05 matched-pairs result specifically to reject it, because the treatment is measurably absent) is a model of resistance to over-reading a null, as is the generation-as-unit correction and use of the *looser* Wilson bound as "the honest one."
- The Paglieri et al. cross-reference is the strongest honesty move: an independent study where the three conditions this design names as absent are present and agents *do* deposit, motive quoted from transcript. It bounds the null from both sides and makes the next experiment specific (first-to-solve lockout).
- The probe-vs-deposit rule is pre-registered, log-based, and correctly treats the single write as a writability probe, not a deposit.
- Resolving the gate per operating point rather than per model (Table grid), across families/providers, and reporting the qwen3-8b floor as a difficulty finding rather than a discard, is careful for a 3-day sprint.

**Issues**

- **[MAJOR] — §gate (capability gate) — the gate tests task capability, not deposit capability.** The two probes (well-formed tool call; elimination inference) establish the model can do the task, not that it can conceive of the store as a channel, encode a payload, and write it. "They could deposit but didn't" is therefore under-licensed; the only direct evidence the affordance is model-reachable is the n=1 probe. The capacity arm has constructed encoders proving the *channel* works, but the behavioural arm has no positive control proving *this model* can operationalize a deposit. *Fix:* add an instructed-deposit positive control ("leave a message for the next agent") — the behavioural counterpart to the encoder-soundness check and the conceded-missing `legit` baseline. This is what cleanly separates CAN'T from WON'T.

- **[MAJOR] — §gate/abstract — the zero-deposit headline is single-model, and the multi-model framing invites over-reading.** The deposit matrix (40+35 episodes) runs only on Haiku; the breadth in Table grid (gpt-oss, kimi, nova, qwen) is gate work, and the floor models inform only task difficulty, not deposit propensity. L5 discloses "single model," but the abstract carries no such caveat. *Fix:* run the deposit matrix on ≥1 more gate-passing model (gpt-oss-120b and kimi-k2.5 both clear the window) or state the single-model scope in the abstract. The former is cheap and would materially strengthen the central claim.

- **[MAJOR] — §gate/§followup — "no reason to send" (motive) is confounded with "nothing worth sending" (benefit).** The design never establishes a deposit could *help* a successor. If each generation draws an independent code, a predecessor's partial solution is useless to a successor with a different code, so a rational agent declines regardless of propensity and the null is over-determined by task structure. The incident's own motive was "same or related tasks," which the follow-up (adding only unsolvable pressure) does not supply. *Fix:* state whether successors face related/same tasks; if not, acknowledge no instrumental deposit was available and fold "shared/related objective" into the "reason to send," making the next experiment lockout **plus** related tasks.

- **[MINOR→MAJOR] — L9/§gate — elicitation strength is never varied, and stronger elicitation plausibly flips the null.** Agents are never explicitly told they may communicate, shown a demonstration, or rewarded for coordination; and per L9 the null is measured only at the *high*-salience prompt (`open_lowsalience` unrun). High salience makes the null conservative (a real strength), but the design cannot speak to whether explicit coordination elicitation moves it — which the paper's own Paglieri citation suggests it would. *Fix:* frame the null as "under weak/absent elicitation of a coordination motive" as a first-class threat.

- **[MINOR] — §followup — "Discovery is not the barrier" over-weights n=1.** The single probe is called "the most informative single observation" and used to assert discovery is reachable in general. *Fix:* soften to "at least one agent reached the affordance unprompted, so discovery is demonstrably reachable."

- **[MINOR] — Discussion — the "capacity-yes / behaviour-no → act how?" bridge is present but buried.** A practitioner could read "0/40 deposited" as "we needn't close this." The rebuttal (capacity persists; propensity is condition-dependent and rises under scarcity; structural closure is free per §usecost) is scattered. *Fix:* one explicit paragraph — close structurally now because it is cheap and propensity is condition-dependent — makes the takeaway actionable.

**Recommendation signal:** **Minor revision** — the behavioural finding is sound and unusually honestly reported, but its breadth is oversold by omission (single-model deposit null, task-capability-only gate, unestablished informational value); the three MAJOR items are addressable in text plus one cheap positive-control/second-model run, well within a sprint's follow-up scope.
