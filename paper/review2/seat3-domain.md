## Reviewer 3 — Domain: Covert Channels & Security

**Summary assessment**

This revision is, from the covert-channel/security angle, substantially stronger than a first submission would be, and the domain framing is now largely correct rather than merely evocative. The paper grounds its claim in the right canon — Lampson's confinement problem, Kemmerer's shared-resource-matrix method, TCSEC's deliberate separation of covert-channel analysis from access control, and Goguen–Meseguer non-interference — and, crucially, uses that canon *precisely*: it defines the channel relative to a policy (non-interference the substrate does not enforce) and distinguishes it from the DAC permissions that authorise every operation. The new IFC/DIFC engagement (Denning's lattice, Myers–Liskov DLM, HiStar) is adequate and, more importantly, honestly deployed: it is used to *scope down* the novelty claim to "instantiation + verification for agentic evaluation infrastructure," explicitly conceding that the namespace-closure remedy is a coarse instance of an existing lineage rather than a new principle. That is the right move and it is executed correctly. The zero-error/achievable-rate methodology is positioned correctly as a $C_0 \le C$ lower bound with an honest sampling caveat, and the count/existence double-counting correction is exactly the information-theoretic rigor this kind of capacity work requires.

The remaining problems are additive and precision-level, not structural: one genuinely important missing reference (NCSC-TG-030), the absence of any temporal bandwidth figure while invoking a disposition framework that is historically bandwidth-threshold-defined, an abstract overclaim about *what* produces the zero rung, and a couple of citation-completeness gaps (Flume, Ristenpart). None is fatal. The security claims I checked are sound.

**Genuine strengths**

- **Correct policy-relative framing of "covert."** The statement that the channel is covert w.r.t. a non-interference requirement but *not* covert w.r.t. filesystem permissions (§2.1) is exactly the TCSEC/orange-book distinction between covert channels and DAC, and it is stated more precisely than most security papers manage. This is the paper's strongest domain foundation.
- **The permission-guard result (§5.4, Table `tab:monitors`) is a sound domain claim.** A guard that compiles an allow-list into enforcement cannot, by construction, separate two sequences of individually-permitted operations; AUC 0.50 / TPR 0 is the correct and essentially definitional outcome. This is a faithful empirical restatement of the confinement problem, and the paper concedes it is a construction confirming a definition.
- **Zero-error capacity handled correctly.** The manuscript correctly notes the exhibited code gives $C_0 \le C$, that 8 seeded payloads *sample* rather than *certify* noiselessness, and that every figure is a lower bound. This is more careful than the norm.
- **The count/existence double-counting correction (§5.6/joint).** Recognising that `count` is a deterministic function of the existence pattern and therefore cannot be summed onto `existence` is precisely the classical warning against naively adding channel capacities, and it is used to produce a jointly-measured residual instead of a sum. Correct and well-motivated.
- **The `order` channel (0 measured bits vs. the naive $\log_2(n!)=1684$).** A clean, filesystem-grounded demonstration of the declared-width fallacy, confirmed on real substrates. This is the paper's best single illustration of why "sum the field widths" is not capacity.
- **Co-tenant vs. cross-tenant is handled consistently.** AIS-14 (cross-tenant) and AIS-11 (inter-agent, co-tenant) are correctly separated, and the paper stakes its ground squarely in the co-tenant gap where authorization controls are silent. "No tenant boundary was crossed at the point the channel formed" is the correct characterisation of the incident.
- **The IFC/DIFC scoping is defensible.** The right references are now present, and the novelty claim is narrowed to what the evidence supports.

**Issues**

- **[Major — §2.1/§2.2, appendix clauses, references.bib] Missing NCSC-TG-030 (the "Light Pink Book," *A Guide to Understanding Covert Channel Analysis of Trusted Systems*).** This is the definitive covert-channel *analysis and bandwidth-handling* guideline, and the paper's entire disposition ladder (closed / monitored / accepted) plus its "measure capacity, don't declare it" thesis sit directly in its lineage — yet it is uncited. TCSEC's own covert-channel bandwidth thresholds (the ~100 bps audit / ~1 bps consideration guidance) are likewise not surfaced despite `tcsec1985` being cited. *Fix:* add NCSC-TG-030, and position the close/monitor/accept ladder explicitly as the agentic-eval instance of that handling guidance. This is the single most defensible missing citation and it is central, not decorative.

- **[Medium — throughout §5, Table `tab:ladder`] No temporal bandwidth; the invoked disposition framework is historically rate-defined.** The classical accept/monitor/close disposition (TCSEC/NCSC-TG-030) is keyed to bandwidth *thresholds in bits per second*. The paper reports bits-per-event and total bits-per-deposit and never converts to a rate, so a defender cannot map its residuals onto the threshold framework it echoes. The paper *has* a good defense — for a message-board channel a single deposit carrying a whole key is the security-relevant event, so capacity-per-deposit is arguably the right measure — but it never states this distinction. *Fix:* one paragraph explaining that the operative quantity for a persistent shared store is capacity-per-deposit, not the TCSEC per-second bandwidth, and why the rate-threshold disposition does not govern here.

- **[Medium — abstract line ~51; §5.3 lines ~496–503; §5.4 lines ~550–555] The abstract mis-attributes the zero rung, and the 52-vs-0 residuals need reconciling.** The abstract says "Only closing directory names reaches zero residual." But by the body's own account, `size`+`mtime` reach 0 *not* through name closure but through a separate fact — a fixed, non-extensible skeleton with `mkdir` denied, so there are no writable named slots to modulate. §5.4 even states that structural name closure *alone* leaves 52 bits (size 13 + mtime 39), and only additionally modelling directory presence / denying the directory operation drives it to 0. So the headline zero is "directory-name closure **plus** a fixed non-extensible skeleton," and the load-bearing mechanism (a denied create/`mkdir` on a fixed skeleton, plus the implicit assumption that content closure normalises object length so size cannot leak through redacted objects) is under-specified. A careful reader must stitch three passages together, and the abstract omits the dependency entirely. *Fix:* (a) correct the abstract to name the skeleton/denied-creation condition; (b) state explicitly whether content closure normalises size (otherwise size leaks through redacted fixed-named objects and the zero does not hold); (c) reconcile the 52-vs-0 as the monitoring-vs-structural-closure distinction in one place.

- **[Medium — appendix clauses 2–3] The verification method certifies a lower bound (presence of capacity), not its absence; "closed" is asserted from failing encoders and is game-able.** Clause 2's encode/decode measurement can only *exhibit* residual capacity; "no encoder found" ≠ "channel closed," which is the same non-interference-unprovability the paper concedes in §4.3. An adversarial or merely lazy lab could clear a threshold by publishing a config against which the *released* encoder is weak. The verification framing ("verifiable without the lab's network") is genuinely useful, but it verifies a lower bound, and the "closed" disposition (clause 3) inherits the unprovability. *Fix:* state in the appendix that clause-2 measurement verifies capacity's *presence*, that a "closed" disposition rests on the structural argument of §5.3 rather than on encoder failure, and that the auditor's assurance is bounded by the strength of the released encoder.

- **[Low–Medium — §1/§2 (IFC), references.bib] Citation-set completeness on two axes.** (a) *DIFC:* HiStar is cited but Flume (Krohn et al., SOSP 2007, DIFC for standard OS abstractions) is the natural third alongside Myers–Liskov and HiStar and would round out the "practical DIFC exists but is heavier than eval harnesses adopt" claim; Asbestos (Efstathopoulos et al., SOSP 2005) optional. (b) *Cloud co-tenancy:* Ristenpart et al. ("Hey, You, Get Off of My Cloud," CCS 2009) is the canonical co-residency/multi-tenant-channel reference and situates the co-tenant threat model in the cloud-security literature. *Caveat to state if cited:* Ristenpart concerns *achieving* co-residency and cross-VM *timing* side channels, whereas here co-residency is given by design and the channel is a *storage* channel through permitted operations — so cite it for lineage/context, not as the same channel type. *Fix:* add Flume; add Ristenpart with the channel-type distinction noted.

- **[Low — §2.1, Table `tab:ladder` `mtime` row] Storage-vs-timing taxonomy left implicit.** `mtime` is treated (correctly) as a *stored, sender-influenceable attribute* — i.e., a storage channel in Kemmerer's sense — not a timing channel, even though "timing channel" is the classical partner term and a reader may expect it. One sentence stating that all eight attributes are storage channels (the timestamp is stored state a receiver reads, not a modulation of observed real time) would forestall a taxonomic objection.

- **[Low — §5.4, Table `tab:monitors`] The permission monitor at AUC 0.50 is a degenerate (never-fires) classifier, not a discriminator operating at chance.** Worth one clause so a reader does not read 0.50 as "tuned to the operating point where it happens to be at chance."

- **[Low — §2.3/§4.2] The $C_0 \le C$ gap here is coder inefficiency, not channel noise.** For an essentially noiseless deterministic storage channel, $C_0 = C = \log_2(\text{distinguishable states})$, so the "lower bound" understatement is entirely encoder weakness (the paper already shows content is 8× below the body limit), not a fundamental zero-error penalty. Stating this would sharpen the otherwise-correct capacity framing.

**Recommendation signal**

From the covert-channel/security angle: **accept with minor revisions.** The domain framing is now rigorous, the co-tenant/cross-tenant threat model is consistent, the permission-guard blindness result is sound, and the zero-rung is correctly presented as structural-plus-measured rather than a proof of non-interference. The IFC/DIFC engagement is adequate and the novelty is defensibly scoped to agentic evaluation infrastructure and its verification. The blocking-severity items are all additive: cite NCSC-TG-030 (and surface TCSEC's bandwidth guidance), address the absence of a temporal bandwidth figure, and correct the abstract's attribution of the zero rung so it names the fixed-skeleton/denied-creation dependency rather than crediting directory-name closure alone. Flume and Ristenpart are recommended completeness citations. None of these threatens the paper's core contribution.
