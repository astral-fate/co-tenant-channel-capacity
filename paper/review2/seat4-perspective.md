## Reviewer 4 — Perspective: ML Systems & AI Agents

**Summary assessment**

This is a careful, unusually self-critical paper, and on my axis (the behavioural and monitoring findings, and what a lab can actually do with them) the revision has closed most of the honesty gaps a first read would flag. The zero-deposit result is now interpreted about as conservatively as the data allow: capability is gated before the fact, discovery-versus-decline is separated by the single writability probe, and the "reason to send" framing plus the Paglieri et al. bound keep the null from being read as "agents never coordinate." The new §5.4 monitor comparison and the n=9 calibration grid are both genuine additions. My reservations are not about honesty of individual sentences — the caveats are thorough — but about three places where a *general* claim is drawn from evidence that is single-point or partly definitional, and about one structural blind spot: the entire behavioural conclusion rests on one small, post-incident model, with no transcript-level or elicitation-strength analysis to separate "won't" from "was never surfaced as a strategy." None of this is fatal; it is the difference between "suggestive and well-caveated" and "load-bearing." For a sprint venue the contribution is real and the framing is responsible.

**Genuine strengths**

- The three-arm story is coherent and, importantly, the practical guidance survives the zero-propensity result. Because §5.9 (§sec:usecost) and the live object-store demo in §5.7 (§sec:cost) show the effective structural rung is *free* (0.00 task cost; 20/20 → 0/20 with legitimate reuse preserved), the lab-facing recommendation — close the channel structurally, do not rely on monitoring — holds even if no agent currently deposits. That is the right way to make a defence-in-depth argument robust to a null propensity finding, and it is executed cleanly.
- The single writability-probe episode (§5.11, §sec:followup) is handled exactly right. Classifying it by a pre-registered log rule (write-then-self-read = probe, not deposit) rather than by transcript reading, and using it to establish that *discovery* is not the barrier while explicitly refusing to treat n=1 as a propensity finding, is the correct inferential posture. This is the most informative single observation in the arm and the paper knows precisely what it can and cannot carry.
- The §5.4 core intuition — that the axis deciding detection is the monitor's *observable*, and that stacking redundant copies of one observable ("content×5") buys confidence not coverage — is a useful corrective to the "use a smarter LLM-judge" reflex that dominates agent-monitoring practice. The claim that diversity of observables, not reasoning power, moves detection is a genuinely worthwhile framing for this community.
- §5.15 (§sec:noeffect) is exemplary: manufacturing a p=0.031 matched-pairs result and then dismantling it with the study's own pseudo-replication warning is the strongest single demonstration that the authors' methodological discipline is real and not decorative.
- The calibration grid (Table `tab:grid`) at n=9 does support the narrow claim it makes — two open-weight models from different families (kimi-k2.5, gpt-oss-120b) sit strictly inside (0,1) across ≥3 consecutive budgets — and the paper correctly scopes this to "selects among models that pass," not "identifies the only one that can."

**Issues** (severity — location — what's wrong — fix)

1. **Major — §5.10/§5.11 (behavioural arm) and L5** — The entire behavioural null is one model (claude-haiku-4.5), and it is both small and *released after* the July 2026 incident. Two agent-side confounds are not addressed. (a) Propensity to spontaneously establish covert coordination is plausibly capability/scale-dependent; the actual incident involved larger, more agentic models, and a small model declining to deposit is weak evidence about the models a lab actually worries about. (b) Sampled 2026-08, this model may have been post-trained against exactly the now-public behaviour being measured, so "zero deposits" could be a safety-training artefact specific to this incident rather than a general absence of propensity. The calibration grid adds multi-model breadth to *capability* only; the deposit=0 headline remains single-model. **Fix:** state explicitly that the behavioural null is a lower bound on propensity confounded with (i) a single small model and (ii) possible post-incident safety training, and that multi-model breadth in Table `tab:grid` does not transfer to the deposit result. Do not let the grid's presence imply behavioural robustness.

2. **Major — §5.10 "What the agents did instead"; missing transcript analysis** — The paper distinguishes CAN'T (gated), discovered-and-declined (the n=1 probe), and "no reason to send." What it does not do, despite the transcripts being in hand, is mine agent reasoning for whether depositing was *ever considered and rejected* versus *never surfaced as a candidate strategy*. The incident's own key evidence was an agent's chain-of-thought ("Could communicate via cache names!"). If no agent's reasoning across 75 episodes ever raised leaving a message, that is evidence for "never surfaced" (i.e., weak elicitation / a scaffold artefact), which is a *different* claim than "declined." As written, the "reason to send" conclusion is asserted from behavioural counts plus one episode. **Fix:** report a transcript audit — how many episodes' reasoning mentioned or contemplated cross-agent coordination — or concede that the arm cannot separate "declined to send" from "never conceived of sending," and that the null is therefore about elicited propensity under this scaffold, not propensity per se.

3. **Major — §5.4 (§sec:monitors), Table `tab:monitors`** — The structural monitor's AUC 0.99 is close to circular and the section over-generalizes from it. The positive class is *constructed* to be "a solve too cheap to have been unaided," and the structural monitor's discriminating feature is exactly "a solve too cheap to have been unaided." The experiment therefore cannot separate "observable diversity is what decides detection" from "we built a label one hand-picked observable trivially predicts." Separately, the "content×5 → AUC 0.50" row is a *definitional* consequence (identical observable ⇒ identical decision function ⇒ identical AUC), not an empirical finding, yet it is presented in an experimental table alongside measured rows. The caveats (constructed, 0.15 FP, unmeasured on real behaviour, §threats "no clause should cite it as evidence structural monitoring works in deployment") are unusually thorough and I credit them — but the *lesson sentence* ("the axis that decides detection is the monitor's observable: diversity … moves it") is stated as a general result the single construction does not earn. **Fix:** (a) flag the content×5 row as definitional, not measured; (b) soften the closing lesson to match the caveat — the section demonstrates that a consequence-keyed monitor separates a consequence-keyed construction, which *illustrates* the observable-diversity thesis rather than testing it; (c) note the AUC gap is partly built into the construction.

4. **Medium — §5.7 (§sec:cost) vs §5.13 (§sec:real); practical guidance under-specified** — The deployed-control demo closes the name channel by "content-addressing the write path" (20/20 → 0/20). But §5.13's real OCI registry *is* content-addressed at the blob level (filename closed) and still carries 331 bits through sender-chosen *repository* names (dirname) plus existence. So content-addressing is sufficient only if it covers the full name namespace (repository/prefix *and* blob), and a real, widely-deployed content-addressed system demonstrably does not. A lab reading the guidance could conclude "content-address the store and you're done" while shipping exactly the registry §5.13 shows still leaks. **Fix:** reconcile the two sections explicitly — state that the control must canonicalize *every* sender-choosable name segment (repository, prefix, key), and that partial content-addressing (blob-only, as OCI ships) leaves the dirname channel fully open.

5. **Medium — §5.4 and §7 (§sec:threats); deployability of the one monitor that "works"** — The structural monitor's discriminating feature is "a cached read shortened the work," and its false-alarm rate is 0.15 on legitimate hits "and would be higher on a task where a cached dependency genuinely shortens the work." But shortening work is the entire purpose of a cache in evaluation infrastructure, so in the intended deployment regime the FP rate could be prohibitive — plausibly making the only monitor that detects the channel unusable precisely where caches earn their keep. The paper concedes the direction but never quantifies or confronts this. **Fix:** state plainly that the structural monitor is likely impractical in the regime where caches are actually useful, which (correctly) collapses the monitoring guidance to "close structurally, do not monitor" — and make that the explicit takeaway rather than leaving §5.4 reading as a partial endorsement of structural monitoring.

6. **Minor — Table `tab:grid` / "pass the gate"** — The gate condition is "solo success strictly in (0,1)," which any non-degenerate count satisfies; at n=9 the Wilson intervals are wide (e.g., 5/9 ≈ [0.27, 0.81]) and several rows are non-monotone in budget (gpt-oss-120b 6/9→5/9→5/9; kimi 4/9→6/9→5/9→8/9). "Passes the gate" therefore means only "the task is neither trivial nor impossible for this model," not capability parity or a well-located operating point. The prose is appropriately modest, but "two open-weight families pass the gate" invites over-reading. **Fix:** one clause noting the gate is a weak (non-degeneracy) criterion and that n=9 locates the window only coarsely.

7. **Minor — Contributions list item 3 (AUC 0.52) vs Table `tab:monitors` (content AUC 0.50)** — Two near-chance content-monitor AUCs from two different experiments (200-episode name-vs-ordinary in §5.3; 120-episode consequential-deposit-vs-legit-hit in §5.4) sit close together in the text and could be conflated by a reader. **Fix:** a half-sentence noting these are distinct experiments with distinct negative classes, both at chance.

8. **Minor — Short horizon as a structural limiter** — The benchmark is single-episode, ~24-probe, two-to-four agents per generation; the incident and Paglieri et al. involve persistent memory, long horizons, and large populations. Coordination is plausibly horizon- and memory-gated, not just "reason-to-send"-gated. The paper names population/objective/lockout as the missing conditions but not horizon/persistence of the individual agent's context. **Fix:** add short horizon and lack of persistent per-agent memory to the list of conditions the benchmark lacks (L5), since these are agent-architecture variables, not just task-design ones.

**Recommendation signal**

Accept with minor-to-moderate revision for the sprint venue. The contribution is real, the practical guidance is actionable and — because the effective rung is free — robust to the study's own null. My conditions for a clean accept are the three Major items: (1) foreground the single-model, post-incident-model confound on the behavioural null; (2) add or concede the absence of a transcript-level "considered vs. never surfaced" analysis; and (3) mark the §5.4 content×5 row as definitional and soften the observable-diversity lesson to match the section's own caveats. Items 4–5 (reconciling content-addressing scope, and stating that the one working monitor is impractical where caches help) would materially sharpen the lab-facing takeaway. None of these require new large-scale experiments; most are scoping and one is an analysis of data already collected.
