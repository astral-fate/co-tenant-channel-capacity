## Devil''s Advocate Report

Seat 5 — Devil''s Advocate. Venue: AI Incident Response Sprint, Track 1 (Containment). Target: `paper/main.tex`, ~1886 lines (revised, experiment-heavy). Mandate: attack the core arguments as hard as honestly possible.

Headline judgment: **no CRITICAL findings survive honest scrutiny — and that is itself a finding.** The paper pre-concedes almost every knockout blow (L1–L9, the §5.3–§5.4 caveats, threats-to-validity). The revisions do not break the title thesis; they mostly re-derive it while adding surface area, circular constructions, and a truism sold as a discovery. The sharpest damage lands on the NEW sections, not the spine.

**Strongest counter-argument** (~250 words)

The paper''s revisions add experiments that mostly restate what their own constructions guarantee. §5.4''s structural monitor reaches AUC 0.99 by keying on "a solve too cheap to have been unaided" — but the deposit class was *built* to solve cheaply and the legit class was built to "still did the work." The monitor''s discriminating feature is definitionally the construction axis, so the AUC measures the construction, not the channel. On any task where a cache genuinely helps legitimate users — i.e., the reason caches exist — both classes solve cheaply and the separation collapses toward chance. The 15% false-positive caveat is the leading edge of that collapse, not a bound on it. The `content×5` row (AUC 0.50) is arithmetic, not measurement: five copies of a definitionally-blind monitor are blind a priori. So the marquee framing — "diversity of observables, not reasoners, moves detection" — is a definitional truth (a monitor sees only its observable) dressed as an empirical finding. Meanwhile §5.6''s `20/20→0/20` on S3 restates the definition of content-addressing: derive the name from content and the sender cannot choose it; the informative half is the 20/20 *baseline* (the channel is real on a commercial store), not the 0/20 treatment. And the paper''s own OCI evidence — repo names carrying 331 bits under content-addressed blobs — is a counterexample to the S3 demo generalizing. Stacked against a crisp 5-item contribution spine, these additions inflate QA risk without adding a claim the spine did not already carry. The core title thesis survives; the new sections largely re-derive it.

**Issue list**

1. **MAJOR — circularity / construct validity — §5.4 (`sec:monitors`), Table 2, lines 557–611.**
   Challenge: The structural monitor''s AUC 0.99 is circular. The deposit class was constructed so the reader "solved with far fewer probes than an unaided agent needs" and the legit class "still did the work"; the monitor''s sole feature is "a solve too cheap to have been unaided." Feature = construction axis. The experiment establishes only that *if you build two classes that differ on axis X, a monitor observing X separates them.* That is close to tautological. Worse, the design chose a task where the cache does **not** genuinely help legit users; on a task where it does (the normal case), legit hits also solve cheaply and AUC collapses toward 0.50. The 15% FP caveat gestures at this ("that rate would be higher") but never quantifies the collapse, so the reader is left with 0.99 as the salient number.
   What would rebut it: an AUC on a task family where legitimate cache reuse *genuinely* shortens the solve, showing the structural monitor retains separation (or a stated, non-trivial floor). Absent that, the framing should drop from "the axis that decides detection" to "an illustration on separable constructed classes."
   Anticipation: The paper concedes propensity/real-behaviour is unmeasured and says "the lesson is not that structural monitoring is the answer" — this hedging caps the severity below CRITICAL, but the concessions do not remove the circularity from the AUC still reported.

2. **MAJOR — truism-as-finding — §5.4, `content×5` row (AUC 0.50), lines 578, 594–596.**
   Challenge: Five copies of a monitor sharing one observable, over benign bodies that are "independent draws from one pool" so "any separation the monitor finds is separation it invents," are blind by construction. AUC 0.50 is guaranteed a priori; presenting it as a measured row with an AUC column implies experiment where there is arithmetic. The reframing "diversity of observables, not reasoners, moves detection" is the definition of an observable (a monitor detects only what it can see), not a discovery. §5.3 already states the blindness "follows from the monitor''s definition."
   What would rebut it: show a case where redundant same-observable monitors *do* add coverage (they cannot), or reframe §5.4 explicitly as a conceptual argument rather than a result. As stated it is padding that inflates the experiment count.

3. **MAJOR — near-tautology / generalizability — §5.6 deployment in `sec:cost`, lines 684–698, vs OCI evidence §5.10, lines 994–1004.**
   Challenge: `20/20→0/20` closure via content-addressing restates the definition (§3, lines 256–260: derive names from content and "those two rows would collapse"). The treatment half measures nothing non-obvious. The informative half is the 20/20 baseline (channel real on a commercial store) and "zero cost to legit reuse" — but zero cost to legit dedup is what a content-addressed cache is *for*. More damaging: the S3 demo works only because an S3 key is flat, so content-addressing closes dirname+filename jointly. The paper''s own OCI result shows real registries content-address *blobs* while leaving *repository/dir* names sender-chosen (331 bits, `GET /v2/_catalog`). So "content-addressing the write path" is not a general name-channel closure — the paper supplies its own counterexample and does not reconcile it with the S3 headline.
   What would rebut it: run the same control on the OCI registry (or any store with hierarchical sender-chosen names) and show it also reaches 0; or explicitly scope the S3 result to flat-key stores and note it does not transfer to the registries the paper itself measured.

4. **MAJOR — scope creep / grab-bag — whole Results section, §5.1–§5.15, ~27 pp for a 3-day sprint.**
   Challenge: The contributions list (lines 108–138) is a crisp 5 items. But the body carries ~13 distinct experimental threads (ladder, joint residual, content-blindness, four-monitor comparison, cost/mediation, S3 deploy, use-cost, payloads, real-infra triple probe, fraction sweep, matrix, follow-up run, negative control, model-screen grid). Most of the added mass (§5.4, S3, use-cost, payloads, model screen) is **not** in the contributions list — it is supporting material that ballooned. The behavioural arm consumes ~40% of Results to conclude, by the paper''s own words, that Δ is "undefined for this configuration" and that a spurious p=0.031 must be rejected — i.e., essentially nothing about propensity. More numbers = more reproducibility/QA risk in a build-substituted pipeline.
   What would rebut it: a demonstration that each thread is load-bearing for a stated contribution, or a restructure that demotes §5.4/payloads/model-screen to an appendix. For the venue, the crisp thesis would land harder at half the length.

5. **MAJOR — internal tension — detector vs. zero propensity — §5.4 / §5.13–§5.14, L5.**
   Challenge: The paper simultaneously reports (a) 0/40 and 0/35 real deposits with propensity "unmeasured," and (b) an elaborate detector characterised at AUC 0.99 on *constructed* deposits. Building a detection method for a behaviour measured at zero, on data whose separability was injected, reads as answering a question the paper argues nobody currently asks. Capacity work and detection work can coexist, but §5.4 presupposes the very behaviour §5.13 measures at zero.
   What would rebut it: frame §5.4 strictly as "what observable a *future* detector would need," not as a monitor comparison with an AUC leaderboard; or defer it until a non-zero deposit rate exists to characterise against. The paper''s bridge sentence (lines 609–611) concedes this but leaves the AUC table standing as the section''s visual centre.

6. **MINOR — headline built from self-chosen constants — abstract + §5.5, "148.7× / 3075 bits."**
   Challenge: 3075 bits and the 20.7-bit passcode are both the authors'' constants; §5.1 concedes content=8192 is "eight times below what the substrate offers" and filename/dirname use "an alphabet we chose to be conservative," and §5.12 shows the real body limit is 8192× larger. The headline ratio is theatrical. §5.9 (payloads, third-party secret sizes, 5/5 fit) is the genuine rescue and should carry more weight than "148.7×."
   What would rebut it: lead with "the residual carries every externally-specified secret we tested, up to a 256-bit key" (§5.9) rather than a multiple of two chosen numbers. Already half-done; the abstract still leads with 3075.

7. **MINOR — abstract omits load-bearing caveats — abstract, lines 39–56.**
   Challenge: "still leaves 3075 bits… only closing directory names reaches zero… verifiable by an outside auditor without the lab''s network" is stated flatly. The body concedes the residual is synthetic (L3), a lower bound (L1), coder-dependent (§5.1), and that the auditor verifies the *published spec*, not the running system (lines 803–806). "Verifiable by an outside auditor" is true only for spec-compliance.
   What would rebut it: one clause in the abstract flagging "on a synthetic substrate" and "spec-level verification." Normal abstract compression, but the confidence gap is real.

8. **MINOR — displayed-quantity inconsistency — Table 5 (`tab:cost`), content rung "Bits 3261."**
   Challenge: The central methodological rule is "no headline is a sum" (§5.2, line 339), yet Table 5''s "Bits" column shows 3261 (the per-attribute *sum*, censored ≥) for content, not the residual 3075, with no `≥` and no note. A reader who internalised §5.2 is tripped.
   What would rebut it: label the column "per-attribute sum (≥)" or substitute the residual.

9. **MINOR — thin certificate for wide channels — §4.2, 8-trial zero-error criterion.**
   Challenge: 8/8 exact recovery certifies a 3075-bit code by sampling 8 points of a 2^3075 input space. The paper concedes it does not certify noiselessness (lines 216–223), but 8 is a weak certificate and the "lower bound" framing is in mild tension with the possibility of an over-credited flaky code (which would make a width an *over*statement, not a floor).
   What would rebut it: report trial counts scaled to width, or a per-carrier failure-probability bound. Adequately flagged, not resolved.

**Ignored alternatives**

- **Per-agent / per-episode namespaces as the primary deliverable.** The Discussion (lines 1550–1556) states namespaces are the *real* fix that "convert the flow into an ordinary access attempt." If so, the closure ladder measures a problem the namespace fix dissolves. The paper never argues why a lab should adopt the ladder over simply giving each episode its own namespace — which harnesses arguably should do regardless. The whole capacity apparatus may be instrumenting a design smell the recommended fix removes wholesale.
- **Write-volume rate-limiting / anomaly detection** as a containment control is never considered, despite the incident''s 70,000 messages implying detectable write volume. A volumetric monitor is a natural fourth axis and would sidestep the name-channel blindness entirely.
- **Well engaged:** the Paglieri "designed-channel" alternative (§6.1) is confronted directly and fairly — a genuine strength.

**Missing stakeholders**

- **The platform/harness engineer who bears the migration cost.** The paper notes "every consumer that constructs a path by name has to migrate" (line 507) and then *explicitly declines to cost it* ("It is not engineer-weeks"). The party who actually pays for dirname closure is written out; "0.00 task cost" prices the agents, not the humans who must rewrite every path-constructing consumer.
- **The legitimate-cache user under the structural monitor.** §5.4''s 15%+ false-positive rate falls on agents doing honest cache reuse on tasks where the cache helps. Their cost is a number, not a represented stakeholder whose workflow the recommended-against monitor would degrade.
- **The auditor''s real position.** The paper leans on "verifiable without the lab''s network" but concedes the auditor can only check the *published* config, not the running system (lines 803–806). An auditor who cannot detect spec≠runtime is weaker than the abstract implies.

**Observations (non-defects)**

- The negative-control section (§5.15) — reporting a real p=0.031 specifically in order to reject it, and naming the three mundane explanations including the paper''s own pseudo-replication — is exemplary scientific hygiene and rare. Acknowledged without reservation.
- The behavioural null is handled honestly: "Δ is undefined for this configuration rather than small," and the follow-up run (§5.14) correctly identifies that the first matrix never drew an unsolvable task. The paper resists spinning a null into a finding.
- The single writability-probe episode (§5.14) is correctly classified by a pre-registered rule rather than by transcript-reading, and correctly labelled n=1 hypothesis.
- The fraction-vs-residual argument (§5.16 / `sec:ratio`) is genuinely good methodology and the ceiling sweep (Table 9) makes the point concretely.
- The paper anticipates most of my attacks (§5.4/§5.13 propensity caveats, L3 synthetic, L4 constructed, §3 naming-assumption scope, L5–L9). The severity ceiling of this review is a direct consequence of that self-awareness: it is hard to land a knockout on a paper that has already conceded the vulnerable ground. The residual criticism is that the *rhetoric* (AUC 0.99, 20/20→0/20, "diversity of observables moves detection," 148.7×) repeatedly outruns what the conceded-as-circular or conceded-as-synthetic experiments establish.

**Verdict for this seat:** The core thesis — "closing content does not close the channel" — is sound, well-hedged, and survives adversarial reading. The revisions (§5.4, §5.6, model screen) add length and QA exposure while mostly re-deriving the spine through constructed or definitional results; two of them (§5.4 circularity, S3 non-generalizability vs the paper''s own OCI evidence) would benefit from removal, demotion, or a stated scope limit before the growth is a net gain rather than dilution.
