## Reviewer 1 — Track-Fit

**Summary assessment**

The paper measures the covert-channel capacity that survives progressive "closure" of a shared package cache used by co-tenant evaluation agents, using working encoder/decoder pairs under a zero-error criterion rather than declared field widths. Its headline is that closing file *content* — the control a payload-inspecting monitor implements — still leaves ~3075 bits carried by entry names, size, existence and timestamp, and that only closing *directory names* reaches zero, at zero measured task cost. It pairs this with a behavioural arm (agents almost always read the shared store, never deposited) and an appendix of six concrete AIS-11 verification clauses. For this venue the fit is excellent and the adoptability case is unusually concrete and honest; my concerns are about emphasis and coherence, not substance — the paper systematically *under*-sells the one contribution (a free, externally verifiable containment control) that this venue rewards most, and it does not resolve the tension between its own null behavioural result and its "adopt this now" argument crisply enough for a judge skimming on impact.

**Genuine strengths**

- **Track-1 fit is as tight as it gets.** The incident used directory names as the carrier; the paper's terminal rung is directory-name closure. It quotes the METR reasoning trace ("we could leave/find messages in WebDAV MKCOL directory names," lines 79–81) and the OpenAI post-mortem's reestablishment-by-directory-names passage (lines 267–272). The connection to the July 2026 incident is not analogical — it is the same channel. This is exactly Track 1 (containment/contamination).
- **The "would a lab adopt it" bar is met better than most submissions will manage.** The proposed control (dirname closure) is concrete, is shown to be *structural* rather than payload-class (§Cost, lines 581–588), costs 0.00 task success at every probe budget (Table `tab:usecost`, lines 705–715), and is externally verifiable without the lab's network by re-running a stdlib-only script against a published config (lines 668–683). The appendix operationalises this as six AIS-11 clauses each with evidence + cost (lines 514–564). That is precisely the artefact a lab could pick up.
- **Honesty is exemplary and venue-appropriate.** Novelty for capacity measurement is explicitly disclaimed and priority is conceded to Metere (lines 195–199). The residual-not-fraction convention is argued, not assumed (§`sec:ratio`). The behavioural null is reported as *undefined, not small* (line 16), and the negative control (§`sec:noeffect`) is presented "in order to reject it." A judge scoring rigor and integrity will reward this.
- **Positioning against related work is precise.** Motwani (content-steganography) and Huang (observable events) are cleanly distinguished on the channel axis (lines 174–204), and the CSA AI Controls Matrix gap (AIS-11 states policy, attaches no verification method) is the correct hook for the contribution.

**Issues**

- **MAJOR — Abstract (lines 39–58) and Title (lines 30–32) undersell the adoptable control, the axis this venue weights most.** The abstract's second half spends its words on the behavioural null and the writability-probe anecdote ("One probed the store's writability unprompted"), while the actionable result — *structural directory-name closure reaches zero residual at zero measured task cost and is checkable by an outside auditor* — never appears. The title names the measurement finding, not the containment control. A reader scanning titles/abstracts at a containment track will not see that a cheap, deployable control is on offer. *Fix:* add one or two sentences to the abstract stating the control result and its verifiability, and consider a title that signals the remedy (e.g., a subtitle foregrounding "namespace/dirname closure" as the containment move).

- **MAJOR — the paper's own behavioural arm points against its adoption case, and the reconciliation is buried.** §`sec:gate`/§`sec:followup` find 0 deposits in 40 and 35 episodes; a skeptical lab can read this as "agents don't use the channel, so I need not close it." The counter-argument (the incident and Paglieri et al.'s 100-agent swarm show deposits *do* happen given a reason to send; lines 95–123, 353–369) rests entirely on external evidence, while this study's own data cuts the other way. This central capacity-vs-behaviour tension is resolved only implicitly and late. *Fix:* state the resolution explicitly and early (end of Introduction or top of Discussion): capacity is a substrate property that exists regardless of current propensity; propensity is config-dependent and demonstrated at scale elsewhere; therefore the control is warranted as containment-in-advance. One paragraph up front prevents the null from being misread as "no problem here."

- **MINOR/MAJOR — there is no consolidated "what a lab should do" statement.** The adoptability content — mediation counts, enforcement class, task-cost, external checkability, standards clauses — is spread across §`sec:cost`, §`sec:usecost`, §`sec:real` and the appendix. For a venue judging adoption, the reader has to assemble the recommendation themselves. *Fix:* add a short, boxed "Recommendation" subsection in the Discussion: the specific closure to deploy, its cost, how to verify it, and the caveat that the number must be re-measured per substrate. This is a small edit with high payoff for the adoptability score.

- **MINOR — the quotable residual (3075 bits / 148.7×) is synthetic and non-transferable, and the abstract does not flag this.** §`sec:real` (lines 796–923) is admirably candid that the object store has a *different* ladder and that Table 1's numbers do not transfer without re-measurement; L3 concedes the substrate is a model. But the abstract states "3075 bits ... 148.7 times the task passcode" with no synthetic qualifier, inviting a reader to quote it as a production figure. *Fix:* add "on a synthetic model of such a cache" (or similar) where the residual first appears in the abstract, consistent with the care taken in the body.

- **MINOR — scope is over-built for a 3-day sprint, and the two arms compete for the spine.** The manuscript (~1719 lines) carries a capacity arm, a behavioural arm, real-infrastructure probes (Docker volume, OCI registry, S3-class store), a negative control, and a six-clause standards appendix. Each is honestly scoped, so this is not an overclaiming problem — but the behavioural machinery (gate, calibration, screen, follow-up, negative control) occupies a large share of the paper while delivering mostly null/negative results, which dilutes the crisp containment message the venue rewards. *Fix:* signpost the two-arm structure explicitly at the end of the Introduction ("Arm 1 measures what the substrate can carry; Arm 2 asks whether agents in this configuration used it"), and consider compressing the calibration/screen detail (Tables `tab:screen`, `tab:grid`, `tab:calib`) into one table plus an appendix pointer so the capacity+control spine stays foregrounded.

- **MINOR — Contribution 5 (lines 122–126) lists a negative control as a contribution.** Intellectually this is defensible and even admirable, but framed as a headline "contribution" alongside the measurement it can read to a fast judge as padding the list with a non-result. *Fix:* either recast it as a methodological safeguard ("a self-check demonstrating the design can manufacture a spurious p<0.05, motivating the report-the-constraining-quantity discipline") or move it out of the enumerated contributions into the methods framing.

**Recommendation signal**

**Minor revision.** Excellent Track-1 fit, a genuinely adoptable and externally verifiable control, and exemplary honesty; the fixes needed are framing/emphasis — surface the free, verifiable containment control in the abstract/title, reconcile the behavioural null with the adoption case up front, and consolidate a lab-facing recommendation — not new experiments.
