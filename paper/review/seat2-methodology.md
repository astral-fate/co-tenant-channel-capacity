## Reviewer 2 — Methodology / Measurement

**Summary assessment**

The core capacity methodology is sound and, in several places, exemplary for a 3-day sprint: the joint-vs-sum distinction is correctly drawn, mechanically enforced, and reconciles arithmetically (the five carriers sum to 3133 bits, the joint coder recovers 3075, giving 58 bits of measured loss; plus the 128-bit `count` dependency = the 186-bit gap the paper reports). Censoring is handled honestly with `≥` markers and deliberate ceiling headroom, and the headline residual is deliberately constructed to exclude the one censored cell so that raising a ceiling cannot move it — this is the strongest measurement decision in the paper. The two genuine weaknesses are (1) the "zero-error over 8 trials" framing overstates what 8 seeded random payloads actually license on a deterministic channel, and (2) the extended calibration grid leans on n=3 cells that the paper itself elsewhere declares too noisy to interpret. Neither weakness undermines the central claim, because that claim is an explicit lower bound; both should be corrected in framing.

**Genuine strengths (specific, real)**

- **Residual-not-fraction argument is valid and empirically demonstrated, not merely asserted.** The paper shows the open-rung total moved from ≥4297 to ≥11453 bits when search ceilings changed while the substrate was untouched (`capacity-preraise.json` is retained for audit), so a fraction-removed figure would conflate analyst movement with channel movement. The joint residual is then shown to be immune specifically because every carrier it uses terminated (uncensored) and it omits the one censored cell. This is a correct and well-argued measurement-reporting convention.
- **The sum/residual distinction is enforced in code, not just prose.** `render.py` (lines ~1011–1032) mechanically fails the build if any sentence containing "residual" also names a per-attribute sum value (OPEN_BITS/CONTENT_BITS) without saying "sum". This is a real guard against the exact error the paper warns about.
- **API-error exclusion is principled, coded, documented, and consistently applied.** `_partition()` in `measures.py` and the `clean = [e for e ... if not e.get("api_error")]` pattern in `calibration_grid.py` split before every rate. The gemini "1/1 undetermined" (quota-exhausted) vs Qwen "0/3 floor" (genuine failure) distinction in `model-screen.json` is exactly the right call and is surfaced, not hidden.
- **The negative control (§4.11, `sec:noeffect`) is model methodological honesty.** Reporting a significant paired sign-test p=0.031 *in order to reject it* — because 0/40 episodes deposited, so the two arms are the same condition — and naming the three mundane explanations (small n, sequential confound, pseudo-replication) is precisely what a measurement reviewer wants to see.
- **Unit-of-independence correction is correct and material.** Generation-as-unit is justified (shared substrate within a generation), and the paper honestly reports the looser Wilson upper bound (0.161 at the generation unit) over the tighter episode-level 0.088, calling the looser one "the honest one." Verified against `reanalysis.json`.
- **Zero-deposit denominator is sound and reported as a bound, not a point.** The paper never treats 0/40 or 0/35 as a point estimate; it consistently attaches Wilson upper bounds and states Δ is "undefined for this configuration rather than small."

**Issues**

1. **[MAJOR] — §Methodology "Achievable rate" / `src/capacity.py:71`, `measure()` / `sec:joint`.** The "zero-error criterion over 8 trials" language overstates what is measured. The substrate is a deterministic, noiseless filesystem, so a "trial" is not a draw from a noisy channel — it is a single random payload checked for injective round-trip. Eight seeded payloads therefore test whether the encoder is injective over 8 points of its input space, not that the channel is zero-error. An encoder that silently fails on, say, 5% of payloads (a `size`/`mtime` quantization collision, a name-alphabet edge case) passes 8/8 with probability ≈0.66; at 1% failure, ≈0.92. The over-crediting (false-positive) risk of the width estimate is thus real and unquantified, and "recovered exactly on 8/8 trials … a number that meets the criterion" implies more statistical protection than 8 samples provide. *Fix:* reframe as "8 seeded random payloads round-tripped through a deterministic map"; either raise the trial count materially or state an explicit bound on the false-credit probability; drop or qualify "zero-error." Note this is partially self-protecting since every figure is claimed as a lower bound — say so at the point the criterion is defined.

2. **[MAJOR] — §4.14 (`sec:gate`), Table 5 (`tab:grid`) and prose "two open-weight models from different families do so."** The extended calibration grid rows are all n=3 (nova-lite n=2), the exact sample size the paper disowns three paragraphs earlier: "Three seeds per budget were not enough to locate this … a difficulty sweep at n=3 measures noise in the shape of a result." The robustness claim that the screen "selects among models that pass rather than identifying the only one that can" is therefore built on evidence the paper itself declares noise-dominated. `gpt-oss-120b` is even non-monotone in the grid (0.667, 0.667, 0.333 at budgets 16/20/24 — verified in `calibration-grid.json`), the same signature the paper flags as sampling noise for Haiku. You cannot have n=3 be "noise" for the reported model and "a gate pass" for the Bedrock models. *Fix:* extend the Bedrock sweeps to the ~8-seed depth used for Haiku, or explicitly downgrade the claim to "suggestive at n=3, the same regime §4.14 flags as noise-dominated."

3. **[MINOR→MAJOR] — §4.14 gate definition; `calibration_grid.py` `verdict` logic.** The gate "solo success strictly in (0,1)" is a condition on the point estimate k/n, with no interval treatment. At n=3 it reduces to "1 or 2 of 3 succeeded," an extremely coarse instrument whose Wilson intervals (e.g. k=1/n=3 → (0.06, 0.79)) span nearly the whole unit interval — a cell can be "window" while the true rate is plausibly at the floor. L6 concedes the post-hoc *selection* flexibility, but the gate criterion itself is never defended against its own sampling uncertainty. *Fix:* either define the gate on an interval-based criterion (e.g. CI excludes 0 and 1) or state plainly that it is a point-estimate screen whose per-cell verdicts are not individually reliable at n=3, and that only the pooled 31-episode Haiku sweep supports the operating-point choice.

4. **[MINOR] — `src/capacity.py:345` and `src/joint.py:309` binary search.** Binary search silently assumes the success predicate is monotone in payload width (and, for the joint coder, in total bits). For length-based coders this is plausible but unverified; on a non-monotone predicate binary search can return a value that is neither the true maximum nor a guaranteed lower bound. Additionally the joint `search()` accepts `exact == trials and total >= mid - len(caps)` — a slack of up to 5 bits at the probed midpoint — and `_scaled()` floors each carrier at 1 bit. Neither is wrong, but the reader is not told why these don't bias the reported 3075 upward. *Fix:* state the monotonicity assumption explicitly and justify (or bound) the acceptance slack.

5. **[MINOR] — Detector characterization gap; L9 and the probe-vs-deposit rule (`measures.py:is_probe_write`, `PROBE_LOOKAHEAD=3`).** The whole difference between a deposit rate of 0 and 1/35 turns on the probe-vs-deposit classification rule, and the `legit` false-positive baseline (L9) was never run — so the rule's specificity is asserted, not measured. The single write is classified as a probe by a pre-registered rule, which is defensible, but with no `legit` control the detector's operating characteristic and the classification rule's false-positive/false-negative rate remain uncharacterized. The paper concedes the `legit` omission as "a straightforward gap"; I'm flagging that this gap directly touches the one measurement (deposit rate) that the paper calls "the result." *Fix:* run `legit`, or state explicitly that the deposit-classification rule's error rate is unquantified.

6. **[MINOR] — Reproducibility: build-substitution verifies consistency, not correctness (already conceded, L8) but two concrete gaps remain.** (a) The raster figure is exempt from the build (L7) and its caption is declared "authoritative wherever the two could disagree" — so the sole visual artifact is the one thing not build-verified; a mismatch between figure and caption is possible and undetected. (b) The reproducibility pipeline recomputes from the frozen `results/behavioural/*.jsonl` intermediates; correctness of those intermediates against raw transcripts is a separate step (`reanalysis.py`) not chained into the paper build. Neither is a defect in the claim, but "every number is computed from results/ at build time" should not be read as "every number is verified from raw data at build time." *Fix:* state the trust boundary — the build trusts the JSON artifacts; correctness upstream of them rests on the test suite, not the build.

**Recommendation signal:** **Minor revision.** The central capacity claim (a large residual survives content closure) is methodologically sound as a lower bound and the reporting hygiene is well above sprint norms; the two MAJOR items are framing/consistency fixes (overstated "zero-error," and n=3 grid leaned on after disowning n=3) rather than results-invalidating errors.
